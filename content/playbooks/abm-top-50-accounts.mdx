Account-based marketing works when you spend most of your effort on a short list of companies that are likely to buy soon, instead of spreading it across thousands. Fifty accounts is a good size for a founder. It is small enough to research properly and to reach several people at each company, and big enough that a few slow deals don’t sink your quarter.

This playbook shows how we build that list, from a broad market down to 50 named accounts.

## What makes an account worth a place in your top 50?

Three things, and an account needs all of them.

1. **Fit.** It looks like the customers you already win and keep.
2. **Timing.** Something has happened recently that makes your product relevant now.
3. **Access.** You can identify and reach the people who would buy.

Most lists fail because they only check fit. A company can be a perfect match on paper and still have no reason to talk to you this quarter.

## Step 1. Write down your ideal customer profile

Start from your best existing customers, not from who you wish would buy. Look at the deals that closed fastest and the customers who stayed, and write down what they have in common.

| Attribute | What to note |
| --- | --- |
| Industry and business model | What they sell and to whom |
| Size | Employee range and, if you know it, funding stage |
| Location | Countries or regions you can actually serve |
| Team setup | Which team uses your product, and roughly how big it is |
| Tools | Software they use that signals a fit with you |
| Trigger | What was happening at the company when they bought |

If you have fewer than five customers, use your best sales conversations instead and keep the profile loose. You will tighten it after the first round of outreach.

## Step 2. Build a long list of 300 to 500 companies

Pull every company that matches your profile on firmographics alone, meaning industry, size and location. Data tools such as Apollo, Clay or LinkedIn Sales Navigator make this quick. Don’t judge timing yet. The goal here is a complete starting pool.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Clean before you score">
Remove existing customers, open deals, competitors, partners and companies you have already been in talks with. Scoring them wastes time and causes awkward outreach.
</Callout>

## Step 3. Score every account on fit and timing

Give each company a simple score. Keep it simple enough to apply in a minute per account.

| Factor | Points | How to judge it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong ICP match | 0 to 3 | 3 if it looks like your best customer, 1 if it only roughly matches |
| Hiring for a role your product helps | 0 to 2 | Check their careers page and job posts |
| Recent funding or growth | 0 to 2 | A new round or fast headcount growth in the last six months |
| New leader in the team you sell to | 0 to 2 | New leaders review tools and vendors early on |
| Uses a tool that pairs with yours | 0 to 1 | Visible on their site, job posts or tech lookup tools |

A score of 7 or more out of 10 usually earns a place in the top tier. Signals go stale, so record the date you found each one.

## Step 4. Check you can reach the buyers

For each high-scoring account, find the people involved in buying. At a seed or Series A company that is often small.

- **The decision maker**, usually a founder or head of the team
- **The champion**, the person who feels the problem every day
- **Anyone who signs off**, such as finance, for larger deals

If you can’t find at least two of these people, with a LinkedIn profile or a work email, move the account down the list. A great account you can’t reach is not a top 50 account yet.

## Step 5. Tier the 50 accounts

Not every account deserves the same effort. Split the final 50 into tiers.

| Tier | Accounts | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 | 10 | Deep research, several people per account, personal messages on LinkedIn and email |
| Tier 2 | 15 | Researched first lines, two or three people per account |
| Tier 3 | 25 | Lighter personalisation based on the signal, one or two people |

Review the list every month. Move accounts that went quiet down, and promote accounts that just showed a new signal.

## Step 6. Warm up before you pitch

With a top 50 list, the first touch should rarely be a pitch. Spend two to three weeks becoming a familiar name first.

1. Follow the key people and comment thoughtfully on their posts.
2. Send connection requests with a short note that mentions something specific to them.
3. Share something useful with no ask attached, such as a teardown, a benchmark or a playbook like this one.
4. Then start the conversation, tied to the signal that put them on your list.

## What are the most common mistakes?

- **Picking accounts by logo.** A famous name with no trigger rarely replies.
- **Too many accounts.** 500 accounts is a mass email campaign, not ABM.
- **One contact per account.** If that person ignores you or leaves, the account goes cold.
- **Never refreshing the list.** Signals expire. A list from three months ago is out of date.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why 50 accounts and not 200?

For a founder doing outreach alongside everything else, 50 is the number you can research properly and contact across several people. Teams with dedicated SDRs often run larger lists. Inside our 90-Day Pipeline Sprint, we build a wider gold-tier list of around 200 accounts, because we do the research and outreach for you.

### How often should I update the list?

Check signals weekly and rebuild the tiers monthly. Remove accounts that said no or went quiet for a full cycle, and replace them from your long list.

### What if I don’t have budget for data tools?

You can build a first list by hand from LinkedIn search, company websites and job boards. It is slower, but for 50 accounts it is realistic in a few days.
