LinkedIn content works for founders when it does one job. It makes the people you want to sell to recognise your name before your outreach arrives. Likes and follower counts are a side effect. The real measure is whether people who fit your buyer profile start engaging, replying and taking your calls.

This playbook is the strategy we use for founder profiles, from the profile itself to the weekly rhythm and the numbers we watch.

## Why should a founder post on LinkedIn at all?

Because your buyers check you out before they reply. A prospect who gets your cold email or connection request will often look at your profile first. A profile with recent, useful posts about their problem makes the reply easy. An empty profile makes it easy to ignore you.

There is a second effect. People who engage with your posts are warm leads you didn’t have to find. When the right person likes or comments, that is a reason to start a conversation.

## Step 1. Fix your profile before you post anything

Every post sends people to your profile, so it has to convert. Check these four things.

- **Headline.** Say who you help and what you get them, in plain words. “Helping seed and Series A founders book more sales calls” beats “CEO and visionary”.
- **Banner.** Treat it like a billboard. One line on the outcome you deliver, and your company name.
- **About section.** Open with the problem your buyer has, then how you solve it, then one piece of proof. Write it for the buyer, not for recruiters.
- **Featured.** Pin your best post, a playbook or a case study, so the first thing a visitor clicks is useful.

## Step 2. Pick three content pillars

Pillars stop you from posting random thoughts. Each one should connect to a problem your product solves, so an engaged reader is likely to be a buyer.

| Pillar | What it covers | Why it attracts buyers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The problem | Mistakes, myths and costs your buyers live with | They see themselves in it and engage |
| The method | How you approach the problem, step by step | It shows you know the work |
| The proof | Results, client stories, behind-the-scenes numbers | It makes the next step feel safe |

Rotate between them. A week of only proof reads like an advert. A week of only opinions never gets to why anyone should talk to you.

## Step 3. Post three times a week, in text

Three posts a week is enough to stay visible without running out of things to say or burning out. Consistency matters more than volume, so pick a number you can keep for months.

We start founders on text-only posts. Text is the fastest format to produce, it reads well on mobile, and it puts the focus on your thinking. Carousels and video can come later, once you know which topics land.

<Callout title="A simple structure that works">
Open with one line that names a specific problem or claim. Back it up with a short story, a number or a list. End with a question or a takeaway, never a sales pitch.
</Callout>

## Step 4. Write without spending your week on it

Most founders stop posting because writing takes too long. The fix is to separate thinking from writing.

1. **Once a month, do a 30-minute interview.** Someone asks you about recent calls, objections, wins and opinions. That one conversation holds enough material for a month of posts.
2. **Draft from the transcript.** AI is good at turning a transcript into first drafts. A human editor then makes each one sound like you, cuts the filler and checks every claim.
3. **Approve in batches.** Review the week’s posts in one sitting and approve, edit or reject them. We give founders 48 hours, so nothing goes out without a yes.

## Step 5. Engage before and after you post

Posting alone is half the strategy. LinkedIn shows your posts to people you interact with, and buyers notice who shows up in their comments.

- **Comment on your buyers’ posts every day.** Add something useful in two or three sentences. Fifteen minutes a day is enough.
- **Reply to every comment on your own posts** within the first few hours. Conversations keep a post in front of people longer.
- **Connect with people who engage** and fit your buyer profile, with a short note that mentions what they commented on.

## Step 6. Capture everyone who engages

Every like and comment from the right person is a warm signal. Don’t let it disappear into the notifications tab.

Each week, list everyone who engaged, check who fits your ideal customer profile, and decide on a next step for each fit. That might be a connection request, a direct message, or adding them to your outreach list. Inside our sprint, these engagers flow straight into the outreach that runs from your profile.

## How do you know if your LinkedIn content is working?

Ignore total likes. Track these instead, once a month.

| Metric | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Engagement from your ICP | Whether the right people are paying attention |
| Profile views from target accounts | Whether posts send buyers to your profile |
| Replies to outreach that mention your posts | Whether content is warming up your pipeline |
| Inbound conversations | Whether buyers are starting to come to you |

Give it at least two to three months before you judge the strategy. Content compounds slowly, and the first month mostly teaches you which topics your buyers care about.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I post from my personal profile or the company page?

Your personal profile. Buyers follow people more than logos, and posts from a founder usually reach far more people than the same post from a company page.

### What should I avoid posting?

Generic motivation, engagement bait, and anything you couldn’t back up on a sales call. If it doesn’t connect to a problem your buyers have, it attracts the wrong audience.

### Can someone else write my posts?

Yes, as long as the ideas are yours. Ghostwriting works when the writer starts from your own words, from an interview or your notes, and you approve every post.

### How long before LinkedIn content brings leads?

Outreach often gets warmer replies within the first month, because prospects start to recognise your name. Inbound conversations usually take longer, often two to three months of steady posting.
